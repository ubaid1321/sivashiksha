Teaching Happiness: Why Wellbeing Belongs in the Curriculum

Each morning, children walk into their classrooms with their books and the pressure to do well. Success is usually judged by grades and rankings, but many students quietly deal with anxiety, loneliness, or stress. Schools cover many subjects, but it is worth asking if they also help children learn how to be happy and healthy. Beneath the surface, a child may struggle to fit in, worry about speaking up, or hide their feelings. These hidden challenges can affect learning and overall wellbeing, highlighting the need for a broader approach to education.

True happiness in education is not about shielding children from all difficulties or providing constant pleasure. Instead, it means helping young people understand themselves, manage their emotions, develop healthy relationships, and cope with challenges. These skills are not a luxury in today’s rapidly changing world. They are essential for building resilience and a sense of purpose beyond academics. A curriculum that nurtures these abilities prepares students not only for exams, but for life’s uncertainties and opportunities.

A wellbeing curriculum does not require adding another subject to the timetable. It is an integrated approach that blends with daily lessons through practices like self-reflection, open conversations, and social-emotional learning activities. When children learn empathy, teamwork, and healthy coping strategies for stress and setbacks, they are better able to participate and succeed in all areas of life. These skills also ripple out, making classrooms more inclusive and helping create a culture of respect, trust, and collaboration.
